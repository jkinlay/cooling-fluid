# Cooling Fluid Discovery — Red-Team Cycle 4 (External)

Input: `Cooling_Fluid_Discovery_Project_Specification_v1.0.md` (28 Sep 2026), `FDE_Requirements_and_Backlog.json`, `FDE_Jira_Epic_and_Work_Package_Map.md`, `Cooling_Fluid_Discovery_Red_Team_Log.md`, `Specification_Package_Validation.json`.

Method: independent adversarial review of the package as shipped, across (A) technical feasibility, (B) commercial case, (C) risk and governance. Unlike cycles 1–3, this review attacks the project's premise and its fit to the world, not only the document's internal consistency. Market claims were checked against public sources on 28 Sep 2026. No code, data or AWF runtime was executed.

**Overall verdict: DO NOT DECOMPOSE INTO JIRA YET.** The specification is unusually disciplined about what it does *not* claim, and cycles 1–3 have made it internally coherent. But three questions that decide whether the project is worth building at all are either unasked or scheduled after ~20 work packages of infrastructure. Answer them in a two-week desk spike before any ticket is created.

---

## A. Technical feasibility

| Finding | Severity | Attack | Required correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| R4-A01 | **BLOCKER** | **H1 is very probably false as stated, and this is knowable today.** The profile demands (i) no fluorine, (ii) electrically insulating, (iii) boiling 45–55 °C at 1 atm, (iv) acceptable fire behaviour. Non-fluorinated organics boiling in that window are light hydrocarbons, ethers, esters, ketones and low-MW silanes (cyclopentane ~49 °C, methyl acetate ~57 °C, ethyl formate ~54 °C, acetone ~56 °C, diethyl ether ~35 °C). A fluid boiling at ~50 °C has a flash point roughly −30 to −45 °C: every such compound is a Class IB flammable liquid. Halogenated non-fluorine options (chloro/bromo) fail on toxicity, ozone/GWP and corrosivity. The intersection of (i)–(iv) over *known, purchasable* compounds is very likely empty; the PURE lane cannot succeed on its own terms. The spec lists this as risk D06 but schedules the feasibility map (E04, PHYS-002) behind E01–E03. A chemistry lead can settle H1 for the PURE lane in a week with NIST WebBook and a flash-point correlation, with zero software. | Move the feasibility desk study to M0, ahead of all E01–E03 build. Output: a table of every known compound satisfying (i)–(iii), with flash point, LEL/UEL, autoignition temperature, resistivity and toxicity. If the table is empty or fails (iv), the profile must be revised *before* the platform is built, not after. |
| R4-A02 | **BLOCKER** | **Fire behaviour is left undefined precisely where it decides the project.** Section 03 says "seek a fluid acceptable for the agreed operating and fault conditions" with no test or threshold. In practice the constraint is external: data-centre fire codes (NFPA 75/76), property insurers and hyperscaler procurement will not accept litres-per-rack of a Class IB liquid in a server hall. That is a hard constraint, not a research parameter, and it is absent from the profile. | Add regulatory/insurance acceptability as a hard PASS/FAIL/UNKNOWN constraint with named references. If the honest answer is "flammable fluids are unacceptable at scale," the target changes to either (a) fluorine-*reduced* rather than fluorine-free, (b) elevated-pressure or single-phase profiles, or (c) the NOVEL lane — each of which the spec currently forbids as a "silent relaxation". Make the relaxation explicit and early. |
| R4-A03 | MAJOR | **The ML epic is validated on the properties that least need it.** Section 07 correctly predicts that only vapour-pressure/saturation and one transport property will have trainable data; breakdown, flammability, toxicity and ageing will not. But for purchasable pure compounds, boiling point, density and viscosity are *measured* — Antoine coefficients exist. Predicting them is a lookup. Prediction has value only in the NOVEL lane, which the MUR excludes. H2 therefore tests ML on a problem where the physical baseline should win, and section 16 already anticipates NO_MODEL_PROMOTED. | Either (a) accept that M1's scientific content is a data-curation and constraint-screening tool and cut E06 to a thin baseline, or (b) bring a bounded NOVEL/MIX lane into scope so the model has something to predict. Do not build a leakage-audited benchmark harness to confirm that a lookup table beats a random forest at looking things up. |
| R4-A04 | MAJOR | **H3 is pre-doomed to INCONCLUSIVE at MUR scale.** The replay endpoint is "confirmed feasible-candidate count at terminal budget," with a 20 % relative-gain threshold and a positive paired 95 % interval over 30 seeds. On a reference panel of 20–40 substances, where R4-A01 suggests the true feasible count may be zero or one, the count is a small integer and the interval will never exclude zero. Section 11's own rule ("if baseline count is zero, relative gain is undefined") will fire on the first run. | Drop H3 from M1/M2 unless the candidate panel can be made ≥ several hundred substances with the *decision-relevant* labels — which R4-A03 says it cannot. Reframe E09 as engineering (action ledger, cost accounting, batch selection), not a scientific comparison. |
| R4-A05 | MAJOR | **Two runtimes for one preference.** Mathematica-as-orchestrator plus Python adapters plus cross-runtime golden fixtures doubles the test surface, ties headless CI to a Wolfram Engine licence, and asks AWF agents to write Wolfram Language, where LLM code quality and library coverage are markedly weaker than Python (RDKit, BoTorch, scikit-learn, pymatgen all live in Python). Section 05 gates cross-platform claims well, but never asks whether the split should exist. | Run a one-week spike: implement one operation (`normalize_observations` with the unit-conversion fixtures) in each runtime via AWF, measure agent iterations-to-green and defect count. Decide the primary runtime on that evidence. The likely answer is Python core, Mathematica as a notebook client — which also removes D03. |
| R4-A06 | MAJOR | **The 63-row backlog carries no more information than the prose.** All 63 work packages have identical `validation` text on both acceptance criteria ("Execute the requirement-specific positive, negative and boundary cases…"; "An independent reviewer follows input/output lineage…"), all 63 are `proposed_risk_tier: 2`, and `commands: null` throughout. Section 19 says a READY contract needs an "independently executable acceptance procedure"; none exists. The package validation checks ID uniqueness and acyclicity, not testability. | Before decomposition, write actual positive/negative/boundary case descriptions for the M0 wave (nine packages). If that takes more than a day per package, the requirement is too large. A uniform Tier 2 also defeats AWF's routing: differentiate schema/orchestration (Tier 2) from documentation and fixture work (Tier 1). |
| R4-A07 | MINOR | **The W/4 accuracy rule is arbitrary and probably unattainable where it matters.** 2.5 K MAE on saturation temperature for held-out chemical *families* is beyond published structure-property models (typically 5–10 K); on held-out *substances* within a family it is achievable but, per R4-A03, unnecessary. The rule mixes a decision-theoretic idea (window width) with a metric that does not depend on where in the window the candidates fall. | Replace with a threshold-neighbourhood false-pass/false-reject rate (section 10 already gestures at this) and drop the W/4 constant. |
| R4-A08 | MINOR | **10,000 candidates / 100,000 observations is a mismatch with the profile.** After R4-A01's structural filters (no F, BP 45–55 °C ± model error), the purchasable universe is dozens, not thousands. Sizing the ingestion pipeline for 10,000 encourages building for scale that the science cannot use. | Size for the post-filter universe; state it after the desk study. |

## B. Commercial case

| Finding | Severity | Attack | Required correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| R4-B01 | **BLOCKER** | **There is no commercial case in the package.** No customer, market, competitor, cost envelope, IP position, or route to value appears in 13,300 words. Section 01's stated outcome is "a defensible decision about candidate fluids and the usefulness of the discovery process" — a research result, not a business. The question "if this succeeds, who pays whom for what?" is never asked. | Add a one-page commercial thesis before decomposition: target buyer (fluid manufacturer, system OEM, hyperscaler, licensee), what they receive (a formulation, a dossier, a platform, a consultancy engagement), and the order-of-magnitude cost to reach the first saleable artifact. If no answer survives, the project is a hobby and should be scoped and governed as one — which is a legitimate choice, but a different one. |
| R4-B02 | MAJOR | **The market gap is real — and heavily contested by parties with laboratories.** Public reporting confirms 3M ended Novec supply at end-2025, two-phase immersion growth stalled on PFAS uncertainty, and as of mid-2026 no hydrocarbon two-phase fluid has reached data-centre-scale qualification. Chemours (Opteon SF33 as interim), ZutaCore (committed to PFAS-free two-phase by 2026), Shell/ExxonMobil (synthetic hydrocarbons, single-phase), Syensqo/AGC (PFPE/HFE) and academic groups are all active. The demand validates the *problem*; it does not validate a solo, lab-less, Mathematica-based entrant. Every competitor's edge is exactly what this project lacks: formulation databases, test rigs and qualification history. | Position against incumbents explicitly. The defensible niches for this project are (a) a curated, provenance-audited open dataset (a public good that builds reputation), (b) a screening-methodology publication, or (c) an IP play in the NOVEL/MIX lane licensed to a manufacturer. None of these is what the PURE-lane MUR delivers. |
| R4-B03 | MAJOR | **The application picked is the shrinking segment.** Single-phase immersion held roughly two-thirds to four-fifths of the immersion market in 2024–25 and direct-to-chip is the hyperscaler default. Two-phase immersion is the segment most damaged by the PFAS exit. Choosing it maximises the size of the gap but minimises the number of buyers who still want it filled, and those buyers (ZutaCore, LiquidStack) have already partnered with chemical majors. | Test the single-phase and two-phase direct-to-chip profiles as *first-class alternatives* in the M0 desk study, not as "distinct lanes requiring their own evidence". The chemistry constraints for single-phase (BP > 150 °C, high flash point) are far easier and the market is larger. |
| R4-B04 | MAJOR | **No budget means no go/no-go.** Section 16 mandates budget records but supplies none; section 22 forbids inventing quotes. Correct — but a decision-maker still needs an order of magnitude. A 6–12 candidate pilot with dielectric breakdown (ASTM D877/D1816), flash point (D93/D56), materials-compatibility soak and a two-phase rig comparison at a contract lab is a five-figure GBP exercise at minimum, before any fluid purchase or rig time. | Obtain two indicative contract-lab quotations in M0 (as the spec itself requires of E10) and state the envelope. If the owner will not fund a pilot, say so and re-scope to an offline dataset/methodology deliverable. |
| R4-B05 | MINOR | **Platform reuse is asserted, not designed.** Section 01 says the software "shall support other explicitly versioned application profiles," which is the only route by which a discovery *process* has value beyond one fluid. Nothing in the backlog exercises a second profile. | Add a second, cheap profile (e.g. single-phase dielectric) as an M1 acceptance test of profile-independence. |

## C. Risk and governance

| Finding | Severity | Attack | Required correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| R4-C01 | MAJOR | **Three self-reviews with 30 findings, 30 "Fixed", zero rejected, zero left open.** That pattern is diagnostic of a review that optimised for closure rather than dissent. More telling: none of the 30 findings attacked the chemical premise (R4-A01), the fire/insurance constraint (R4-A02) or the absence of a commercial thesis (R4-B01) — the three things a domain outsider raises first. The reviews were rigorous about the document and silent about the world. The log honestly labels them as self-reviews, which is to its credit, but the README's "three red-team cycles" invites over-reading. | Retain this external cycle in `docs/reviews/`. Before Jira decomposition, obtain one review each from a physical chemist and a data-centre thermal engineer. Track findings the owner *rejects*, with reasons; a review log with no rejections is not evidence of adversarial review. |
| R4-C02 | MAJOR | **Governance is heavy where the project is light, and light where it is heavy.** The spec inherits AWF's full apparatus — closure standards, amendment caps, three streams with independent reviewers, Jira mirroring, Tier 2 everywhere — for a project with no named owner, no budget, no lab, no chemist and, realistically, one part-time principal. Meanwhile the decisions that carry actual risk (profile freeze, fire acceptability, spend authorisation) have "owner role" placeholders and no forcing date. Process cost per work package will dominate delivery for the ~50 M-sized items. | Match governance to the phase. M0 should run as a lightweight desk study with a single owner and a written decision memo; AWF's full lifecycle starts at M1 only if M0 returns a viable profile. Name the chemistry and thermal reviewers (even as advisors) before E00 closes, or record that they do not exist and that all scientific gates are therefore owner-attested only. |
| R4-C03 | MAJOR | **Key-person and time risk is unstated.** 63 work packages (49 M, 14 L-to-split), each Tier 2 with critic + specialist review + owner authorisation, is months of owner review time even at agentic implementation speed. No throughput assumption, elapsed-time estimate or "what does the owner do per week" appears. | Add a capacity assumption to section 16 (owner hours/week, review turnaround) and a first re-estimation checkpoint after the M0 wave; treat schedule slip beyond it as a stop-rule input. |
| R4-C04 | MAJOR | **Safety and regulatory constraints are delegated to a partner who does not exist.** "Chemical handling and apparatus operation remain with the experimental partner" is correct in principle, but there is no partner, and the PURE-lane candidates are low-flash-point solvents (peroxide-forming ethers, neurotoxic n-hexane, etc.). Environmental scope is also thin: "fluorine-free" is treated as the sustainability claim, but VOC regulation, GWP and aquatic toxicity apply to hydrocarbons and esters too, and EU REACH/CLP classification will bind procurement. | Add a hazard/regulatory screen (GHS classification, REACH status, VOC/GWP) to the identity/chemistry layer of section 13 as a first-pass filter. It is cheap, data-rich, and will remove more candidates than any ML model. |
| R4-C05 | MINOR | **Public repository, private ambitions.** The spec, search space and screening logic are now public under the owner's name. That is fine for a methodology/dataset play (R4-B02 a/b) and mildly harmful for an IP play (R4-B02 c), where public disclosure of the search strategy before any filing weakens novelty arguments for later formulations. | Decide the commercial route (R4-B01) before publishing anything about specific candidate families or formulations. Keep candidate-level results private until that decision is made. |
| R4-C06 | MINOR | **Stop rules exist but nothing forces them to fire.** Section 16's STOP_DOMAIN/INCONCLUSIVE/CONTINUE vocabulary is good, but every gate is owner-decided with no external trigger, and sunk-cost pressure after 30+ merged work packages is real. | Bind G1 to a calendar and spend cap set in M0, and pre-commit in writing that an empty PURE-lane feasibility table at G1 triggers STOP_DOMAIN for PURE regardless of how much software exists. |

## What to do next (in order)

1. **Two-week M0 desk study, no software.** Enumerate every known compound meeting the structural profile; attach flash point, LEL, autoignition, resistivity, GHS/REACH status. Do the same for a single-phase profile. This resolves R4-A01, R4-A02, R4-A08 and R4-C04 and probably reshapes the project.
2. **One-page commercial thesis** (R4-B01–B04): buyer, artifact, order-of-magnitude cost, two indicative lab quotes, chosen route (dataset / methodology / IP licence / none).
3. **Runtime spike** (R4-A05): one operation, two runtimes, measured.
4. **External specialist reviews** (R4-C01): a chemist and a thermal engineer, each with licence to reject the premise.
5. Only then: revise the profile (likely to fluorine-reduced, single-phase, or NOVEL-lane), cut E06/E09 to the scope the data can actually support (R4-A03, R4-A04), write real acceptance cases for the M0 wave (R4-A06), and decompose.

The package's greatest strength — its refusal to overclaim — is also its diagnostic weakness: it is so careful about what a negative result would mean that it has not asked whether the negative result is already known. Find out first; it is the cheapest experiment in the whole plan.
